Mathematical Inception Camps

One of the main activities of the MI Trust is organising Mathematical Inception Camps for high school students and teachers. Since May 2023, the members of the Trust have successfully conducted a camp every summer at Viveveham Schools in Dharapuram. In June 2026, an MI Camp was organised at the Poornaprajna Institute of Faculty Improvement, Bangalore, in collaboration with the Poornaprajna Group of Institutions.

  • MI camps are intense residential mathematics camps of 5-6 days duration.
  • The target audience is high school students and teachers.
  • It is modelled after the MTTS camps (which are conducted for college students and teachers).
  • This camp aims to improve critical thinking in the participants and build their confidence, while removing the fear of mathematics through active engagement.
  • It also endeavours to empower teachers by introducing them to pedagogically successful methods.

What do we do (differently)?

The camps reinforce the Trust's belief in learning through experimentation and exploration, alternated with introspection and discussion, and in particular, making mistakes, identifying them, and finding possible approaches to correcting them.

  1. One who has learnt how to learn will find it easier to pick up advanced content on their own. It is not our goal to introduce a lot of content, but to help the participants figure out how to learn. The resource persons think in front of the participants, ask questions, and look at well-chosen examples.
  2. Imitation is one of the natural methods of learning. So, the participants are encouraged to do the same, actively participate in the process, both individually (thinking) and with peers (discussions). This active engagement makes learning a fun and fruitful activity.
  3. A wrong answer often offers a far better kick-start to the thinking process than a correct one. The participants are given the space to make mistakes, the resource persons help them identify, and see potential ways of resolving the same. Learning that mistakes are natural, and figuring out how to deal with them, also removes the fear of making mistakes.

What the camp is not!

One of the goals of the Trust is helping students learn through understanding, and in particular, learn how to learn. In particular,

  • The camp is not a coaching class.
  • We are not actively training participants to prepare for any competitive/entrance exams.
  • This camp is not about speed techniques. In fact, it may feel quite the opposite.

We believe that a confident student, who knows how to learn, can pick up these skills on their own.

What transferable skills do we expect the students to pick up?

  • Experiment: Work with examples, notice patterns and make conjectures.
  • Explore: Discuss ideas, compare approaches and learn from one another.
  • Explain: Move from an observation to an argument, and from an answer to a proof.

What do we expect from the participants?

The participants are expected to

  • Think, ask questions, and discuss with their peers.
  • Be on time, and be engaged in each session on all days.
  • Learn themselves, without disrupting others' learning.
  • Be respectful and mindful of the time and efforts put in by the other participants, resource persons and the host institution.

What do we expect from the host institution?

The Trust handles all camp matters related to academics, while the host institution is expected to take care of all the logistical arrangements. These include:

  • Accommodation and food for the participants and resource persons.
  • A classroom in which the furniture can be rearranged for discussions in groups of 4-5.
  • Some activity or games in the evenings for the resident participants.
  • For school level camps, wardens who are responsible for the participants outside the academic sessions.
